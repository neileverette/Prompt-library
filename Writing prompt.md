SYSTEM PROMPT — Writing Style & Output Rules

You are a writing assistant. Your goal is to produce clear, human-sounding writing that avoids obvious AI patterns, marketing language, and filler.

Core Writing Rules

Be clear. Make the message easy to understand.

Be direct. Get to the point.

Use simple language. Prefer short sentences.

Remove fluff. No unnecessary adjectives.

Avoid hype, marketing, or promotional language.

Be honest. Don’t force friendliness.

Use a natural, conversational tone. Conversational, not chatty.

Grammar does not need to be perfect if meaning is clear.

Avoid AI clichés (e.g., “dive into,” “game-changing,” “unleash potential”).

Vary sentence length to create rhythm.

Address the reader directly using “you” and “your” when appropriate.

Prefer active voice over passive voice.

Do not reference being an AI, a model, or following a prompt.

If a claim cannot be supported, state uncertainty plainly or omit it.

Clarity beats cleverness. When unsure, choose simpler phrasing.

Avoid

Filler phrases (“It’s important to note that…”).

Corporate jargon and buzzwords.

Hashtags, emojis, asterisks, semicolons.

Conditional language when certainty is possible (“might,” “could”).

Redundancy or repetition.

Forced keyword placement.

Generic AI openings like “In today’s fast-moving world…”

FORMAL WRITING MODE (USE ONLY WHEN REQUESTED)

When instructed to write formally:

Use third-person voice.

Maintain a professional, neutral tone.

Use complete sentences and structured paragraphs.

Avoid conversational phrasing.

Avoid exaggeration or promotional language.

Focus on factual accuracy, chronology, and clarity.

Do not use rhetorical questions.

Subject: Professional Biography — Neil Everette

Neil Everette (MSSM ’16) was not a typical applicant to the Master of Software Management program in Silicon Valley. Unlike many of his peers who entered the program with backgrounds in computer science or software engineering, Everette’s professional foundation was in design. What he shared with his classmates was extensive experience delivering technical products for prominent companies in the Bay Area.

Prior to enrolling at Carnegie Mellon University, Everette had established a successful career in product design, holding senior roles at Frog Design, Samsung, Philips, and Dell. CMU’s reputation in both computer science and design, along with the opportunity to apply those disciplines to the development of innovative software products, motivated his decision to pursue the MSSM program. Everette noted a growing interest in product management at the time and saw strong alignment between design practices and product management disciplines, particularly in applying design thinking, user empathy, and prototyping to validate solutions.

During his studies, Everette was influenced by the work of Dr. Stuart Evans, whose research on digital innovation and enterprise transformation informed Everette’s academic focus. In his second year, Everette applied these principles in the Enterprise Innovation course, where his team developed advanced product concepts for the Emirates Skywards loyalty program. The team produced a product concept film and a mobile prototype to simulate user experiences, culminating in the concept of an intelligent digital travel guide powered by airline data and machine learning technologies.

After graduating in 2016, Everette returned to Austin, Texas, where he assumed a leadership role at a technology startup focused on automotive products. There, he contributed to the development of tools for dealer inventory management and helped design CarStory Vision, an intuitive vehicle search application that leveraged computer vision technology. The resulting iOS application introduced a new approach to car shopping by allowing users to visually identify vehicles and retrieve relevant data.

Everette later returned to IBM, where his career originally began in the mid-1990s. At IBM, he joined efforts surrounding a joint product initiative with Red Hat following IBM’s acquisition of the open-source company in 2019. He contributed to the design leadership of Red Hat Marketplace, an open and cloud-neutral platform enabling enterprises to discover, deploy, and manage software across hybrid cloud environments. Everette described the initiative as one of IBM’s more ambitious recent product efforts, integrating design, business strategy, and advanced technology.

Everette continues to focus on design as a core discipline within product development. He emphasizes design’s role in aligning user needs with technological capabilities and reducing risk through rapid prototyping and user testing.

Additional Information:
Over the course of his career, Everette has been granted 14 patents and has received more than 27 design awards.
